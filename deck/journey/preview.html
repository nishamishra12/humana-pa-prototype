A surgeon decides a patient needs a lumbar fusion. The office assembles a packet of clinical documents.
The packet is faxed to the plan. Fourteen pages.
Intake checks the member, the form, and the codes. That is a paperwork check, not a clinical one.
A UM nurse picks it up and reads all fourteen pages.
She hunts for four facts and checks each one against policy.
Then there are three ways out.
Path 1. Every fact is there and every criterion is met. She approves.
Path 2. A fact is missing. She pends the case and sends it back to the provider.
The provider has to work out what is missing.
They find it and resend the packet. It goes through the fax all over again.
Back at intake, the case starts from zero. The clock never stopped.
Path 3. The facts are there but the case is borderline. It goes to a medical director.
Only the medical director can deny. A denial can be appealed, and many are overturned.
Every step is somebody doing their job correctly. The delay is built into the process.
